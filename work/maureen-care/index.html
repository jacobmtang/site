01

Building for an underserved market

For such a common disorder, the coping strategies people are given are hard to use in the moment. Therapeutic advice usually comes as static paper worksheets, or as general guidance from a dietitian or therapist: follow a breathing pattern or slow down your eating. When an urge hits, there's nothing dynamic or all-in-one to guide them through it. The tools are disconnected, and rarely within reach when they're needed.

02

Identifying the ideal user

Maureen is built for users with two overlapping needs.

Therapeutic support, in the moment. People who can't afford therapy, or need help between sessions, get research-validated coping techniques from Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) and Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) right when they need them.

Dietary structure, without the numbers. People who need structure around food, but for whom calorie counting and macro calculations are inappropriate, as they invite hypervigilance and restrictive behaviors.

03

Creating the core interactions

I designed every interaction to feel dynamic and responsive:

  • Press feedback: every tappable element responds to touch with a subtle press-in, dimming slightly or scaling down.
  • Haptics: taps of varying strengths confirm actions across the app, with custom patterns for moments like urge surfing and breathwork.
  • Motion: playful, dynamic animations, from Lottie files to custom ones built with Reanimated. Both run on the native UI thread, so they stay performant.

04

Establishing structure with a design library

I set up the foundations every screen would share. A token layer defines colours by role in both light and dark mode, so one change updates the whole app. Spacing follows a 4-point grid, and layouts are capped at a readable width so they hold their shape on iPad.

On top of that, I built about 60 reusable components, from buttons and sheet rows to switches, so each one looks and behaves the same wherever it appears.

05

Unifying every screen

Because every screen comes from the same design library, the user experience stays cohesive throughout.

06

Designing with restraint

Using AI tooling made it fast to prototype features, so the MVP grew quickly, until it was too much to maintain, features repeated each other and the app's core use case got muddled. After weighing which features brought users the most utility, I ultimately trimmed the core product offering from 17 to 11.

07

Show, don't narrate

My first onboarding sequence opened with voice-over steps explaining Maureen's purpose, expecting users to sit and listen. I cut them.

Rather than invent new patterns, I leaned on ones people are already familiar with:

  • A questionnaire-style intake
  • Progressive disclosure
  • Gentle motion and playful animations

Together, they lower the stakes of answering.

Now the flow captures user signals, their goals and what they struggle with, which populate their to-do list with a starter action plan: small steps like pacing a meal or creating a meal plan, each one introducing a feature. This flow encourages them to revisit and discover the rest at their own pace.

08

Good design has already been done

An important lesson was that I didn't need to reinvent iOS. Referencing Mobbin and Apple's Human Interface Guidelines enabled me to keep every screen grounded in best practices. As the app matured, I moved it closer and closer to native.

For example, the bottom tab bar progressed from a custom build to a fully native Liquid Glass bar, native buttons included. It also brought progressive blurs at the top, which were previously made with custom masked gradient fades. Custom bottom sheets became native form sheets, which were more performant and behave the way iOS users already expect.

09

Cleared for takeoff

For the listing, I led with benefits, not feature names, and wrote in possessive terms to reinforce the app as the user's own. The first three slides, displayed prominently on the App Store, share Maureen's brand teal: one introduces the toolkit, and one speaks to each of the two overlapping user needs, therapeutic support and dietary structure. Later slides add variety, with mockups set in real-world scenes and held in human hands.

10

Outcomes and future plans

Maureen is live on the App Store, with ~60–100 monthly active users. Most features are free; premium unlocks extras like recipe imports, room for more recipes, richer insights and cloud backup.

Next, I'm focused on:

  • Strengthening the value proposition of the premium offer
  • Adding Apple's native review prompt, shown after a successful action once someone has used the app a few times
  • Growing organic reach by publishing more articles online to maximize SEO and AEO
