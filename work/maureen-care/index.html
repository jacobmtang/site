01

Building for an underserved market

For such a common disorder, the coping strategies people are given are hard to use in the moment. Therapeutic advice usually comes as static paper worksheets, or as general guidance from a dietitian or therapist: follow a breathing pattern or slow down your eating. When an urge hits, there's nothing dynamic or all-in-one to guide them through it. The tools are disconnected, and rarely within reach when they're needed.

02

Identifying the ideal user

Maureen is built for users with two overlapping needs.

Therapeutic support, in the moment. People who can't afford therapy, or need help between sessions, get research-validated coping techniques from Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) and Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) right when they need them.

Dietary structure, without the numbers. People who need structure around food, but for whom calorie counting and macro calculations are inappropriate, as they invite hypervigilance and restrictive behaviors.

03

Designing with restraint

Using AI tooling made it fast to prototype features, so the MVP grew quickly, until it was too much to maintain, features repeated each other and the app's core use case got muddled. After weighing which features brought users the most utility, I ultimately trimmed the core product offering from 17 to 11.

04

Show, don't narrate

My first onboarding opened with three voice-over steps explaining Maureen's purpose. I cut them. Now new users share their goals and what they struggle with, and Maureen turns that into a starter action plan: small steps like pacing a meal or creating a meal plan, each one introducing a feature. They can revisit it anytime and discover the rest at their own pace.

05

Cleared for takeoff

For the listing, I led with benefits, not feature names, and wrote in possessive terms to reinforce the app as the user's own. The first three primary slides, the ones displayed prominently on the App Store, share Maureen's brand teal: one introduces the toolkit, and one speaks to each of the two overlapping user needs, therapeutic support and dietary structure. Later slides add variety, with mockups set in real-world scenes and held in human hands.
